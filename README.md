# Same Words, Different Worlds

An interactive language autoethnography by Vincent Iyegbuye for ENGL 1301 at Texas Tech University.

## November 2026 editorial redesign

This website now uses a quieter **paper-and-ink digital essay** direction instead of an AI-illustrated theme. It presents two remembered episodes of language misunderstanding in Canada:

1. When saying “sorry” as an expression of concern was heard as an apology.
2. When a Canadian roommate overheard a joking phone call with my brother and interpreted “I will show you pepper” literally.

The full six-paragraph essay and conclusion remain on the page. Interactive experiences include a reconstructed conversation, alternative interpretations of “sorry,” a contextual Nigerian expression explorer, and an intended/unintended audience switcher.

**Evidence note:** All reconstructed conversations are marked as memories. They are not transcripts or recordings of the original events. The synthetic statement video has been removed from the visible site. The older image/video assets may still exist in the repository but are no longer shown by the page.

## Before the final course submission

- Confirm that a hand-coded interactive website is accepted.
- Record your own 1:30–2:00 Statement of Choices, with a transcript or captions.
- Add authentic audio, photos, or other appropriate personal artifacts, with captions and alt text, to strengthen the multimodal evidence.
- Add peer feedback only if it actually happened.
- Keep all narrative details accurate to memory; avoid inventing a specific game or scene.

## Design references

The editorial approach was informed by examples of student image essays and digital storytelling, particularly the [Multimodal Exchange](https://multi-modal-exchange.com/image/) and its [digital storytelling gallery](https://multi-modal-exchange.com/digital-storytelling/). The design is original and does not reproduce their template code.
